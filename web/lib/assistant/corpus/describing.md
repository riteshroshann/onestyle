## Describing a garment: start with the garment

Begin with what the garment is, then add parts and details. "Kurti chahiye" or "I want a maxi dress" is a perfectly good start. Bespoke knows 47 garment types, from kurtis, anarkalis, lehengas and sherwanis to hoodies, blazers, jeans and jumpsuits. If you want a top and a bottom together, say so: "a sweatshirt and pants" becomes a co-ord set with two pieces. You do not need technical words. Bespoke will ask about anything important that you leave out, and you can always change a row on the order card later.

## English examples that work well

Here are requests that Bespoke reads well. "A navy blue hoodie, oversized, with thumb holes." "Knee length A-line dress in black georgette, square neck, three-quarter sleeves." "Wedding guest lehenga in maroon with zari work, no sequins." Notice that each one names the garment, then a few parts, then fabric, colour or details. Short sentences and commas both work. You can send your request in one message or in several; each new message adds to the design or changes what you said before.

## Hinglish examples that work well

You can write the way you talk. "Kurti chahiye, waist pe fitted, neeche se thoda loose, V neck thoda deep, no embroidery." "Lehenga chahiye shaadi ke liye, maroon, gota patti." "Blouse ka gala gol, baazu chhote, kapda cotton." Words such as thoda (a little), bahut (very), nahi and mat (not), neeche (below) and upar (above) are understood and change how the next word is read. Hinglish and English can be mixed freely in the same sentence.

## Saying what you do not want

Say what to leave out as plainly as what to include. "No embroidery", "not too flashy", "lehenga nahi" and "sequins mat dalna" all work. Bespoke removes the value from the design and will not bring it back unless you ask for it again. This is useful for details a tailor might add by habit, such as heavy work on the border. A negative note on a moodboard picture, such as "not this colour", works the same way for that picture.

## Softening and strengthening words

Small words change how strongly something is meant. "Thoda" and "slightly" soften a value: "V neck thoda deep" sits between a moderate and a deep neckline. "Bahut", "very" and "ekdum" strengthen it. Bespoke attaches these words to the nearest value in the same clause, so keep the soft or strong word right beside what it describes. "Neeche se loose" applies the loose fit to the lower part only, while "waist pe fitted" applies the fitted fit at the waist.

## Describing different parts of one garment

One garment can have different fits and details in different places. Use part words to point at them: neckline or gala, sleeve or baazu, waist or kamar, hip, back or peeche, hem or daman, and lower or neeche. "Fitted at the waist, flared below" gives the upper body one fit and the skirt part another. If you describe the front and the back separately, such as a deeper back neck, Bespoke records them as separate rows.

## Details Bespoke has no word for

Sometimes you want something the list of values does not cover: pockets, a drawstring, lining, a particular print. Bespoke keeps these as notes in plain English on the order card and the tailor sheet, so the tailor still sees them. A note is shown as your own words rather than as a chosen value. If you would like a note to be precise, add a measurement or a picture on the moodboard.

## Changing your mind

Every step can be taken back, so you can undo any change. Before each message, answer, edit or moodboard reading, Bespoke saves the design and the conversation, and a Back button under the latest reply restores the previous state, up to thirty steps. You can also tap any row on the order card to pick a different value. When your own edit conflicts with something a picture suggested, your edit wins.

## What Bespoke asks you

When something important is missing, Bespoke asks a short question and offers options. It asks first about the details that are expensive to get wrong. Neckline depth is asked early because a neckline cut too deep cannot be raised again, and sleeve length and garment length matter for the same reason, since fabric that is cut short cannot be lengthened. Colour and fabric are asked too. Which questions appear depends on the garment: a sweatshirt is asked about hood, crew or turtle necks and about oversized fit, while a kurti is asked about round, V, boat, square or mandarin necks.
