## How to measure: before you start

Use a soft tailor's tape and wear thin, close-fitting clothes or measure over underwear. Stand straight and relaxed, feet together, and breathe normally. Keep the tape snug and flat but not tight, with one finger's room underneath. Ask a friend to help if you can, because it is hard to read your own tape accurately. Write each measurement down in inches or centimetres, and say which one you used. Measure your body, not a garment: Bespoke adds the ease for the fit you choose.

## How to measure your bust

Stand straight with your arms relaxed at your sides. Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest, across the bust points, and keep it level all the way around the back. It should sit flat and snug, not tight. Breathe normally and read the number where the tape meets the end. Wear the type of bra you plan to wear with the garment, because it changes the measurement. This is your bust or chest measurement, called chaati in Hindi.

## How to measure your waist

Find your natural waist, which is the narrowest part of your torso, usually just above the belly button and below the ribs. Bend sideways and the crease that forms is a good guide. Wrap the tape around it, keep it level and snug, and breathe out normally without holding your stomach in. This is your waist measurement, called kamar in Hindi. If you want a skirt or trouser to sit lower, such as on the hip, measure there as well and tell the tailor.

## How to measure your hip

Stand with your feet together and wrap the tape around the fullest part of your hips and seat, usually about eight inches below the natural waist. Keep the tape level all the way around, parallel to the floor, and snug but not tight. Check in a mirror that it does not dip at the back. This is your hip measurement, called kulha in Hindi. It matters most for fitted skirts, trousers, churidars and anything that is close at the hip.

## How to measure your shoulder

The shoulder measurement runs across your back from the edge of one shoulder to the edge of the other, where the shoulder bone ends and the arm begins. Stand straight, find the bony point at the end of each shoulder, and measure along the back in a straight line over the top of the spine. Do not follow the curve of the back by pulling the tape tight. This is your shoulder, or kandha. It sets how the sleeve seam sits, so ask a helper to measure it.

## How to measure sleeve length

Bend your arm slightly and measure from the edge of the shoulder, where the sleeve seam will sit, down the outside of your arm to where you want the sleeve to end: the elbow for elbow length, midway between the elbow and the wrist for three-quarter, or the wrist bone for full sleeves. For a short sleeve, measure to the point you like on the upper arm. Keep the tape loose enough that the arm can move freely.

## How to measure garment length

For a kurti, dress or top, measure from the highest point of your shoulder, next to the neck, straight down the front over the bust to where you want the hem to end. Stand straight and keep the tape vertical. Use a mirror or a garment you already like as a guide: measure the same garment from the shoulder seam to the hem. Common endpoints are the hip, the knee, the calf, the ankle or the floor. Tell the tailor which one you mean.

## Measuring a garment you already own

If a garment fits you well, lay it flat and measure it, then give those numbers to the tailor as a guide. Measure across the chest at the underarm, across the waist at its narrowest, across the hip at the fullest part, the shoulder seam to seam, the sleeve from shoulder seam to cuff, and the length from the top of the shoulder to the hem. Double the flat chest, waist and hip measurements to get the full circumference. This tells the tailor how much room you like, which is what ease means.

## Measurement tips

Measure twice and use the same tape and units each time. Do not pull the tape tight and do not leave it loose. Measure in the same type of clothes and underwear that you will wear with the garment. Measure again if your size changes or the garment is for a different season, and ask your tailor to check at the trial. A tailor's own method may differ slightly from the ones described here, so follow their advice if they give you a different instruction.
