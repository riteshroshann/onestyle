## Sending a design to a maker

When your design is ready, you can send it to a maker, meaning a designer or tailor who has a Bespoke maker account. The maker receives your tailor sheet and your measurements as a request in their inbox. The pictures on your moodboard stay on your device and are not sent. Sending a design is how you ask a maker to look at it and, if they agree, to make it. Sending a design does not buy anything and does not fix a price; the maker decides whether to take it on and discusses the details with you in the chat.

## What happens after you send your design

After you send a design it becomes a request with a status that you can follow. The maker reads it first. They may accept it, ask you for missing details, or decline it. If they need more information they flag what is missing and the request moves to Waiting on details. You answer in the chat, and the maker continues. Once accepted, the request moves on to Being made and then to Ready. You are able to talk to the maker in the chat at every stage.

## Request statuses

A request is always in one of six statuses. New means you have sent it and the maker has not acted on it yet. Waiting on details means the maker has flagged something missing and is waiting for you. Accepted means the maker has agreed to make it. Being made means work has started. Ready means the garment is finished. Declined means the maker will not take the request, and you are free to send your design to another maker.

## What Waiting on details means

Waiting on details means the maker needs something from you before they can go on. It usually means a measurement, a fabric, a colour or a length is missing, or a detail on the sheet is unclear. The maker flags exactly what is missing, so you know what to answer. Reply in the chat with the missing detail, or update your design and send it again. The request continues as soon as the maker has what they need.

## How a maker flags missing details

A maker reading your request can flag the specific details that are missing, such as sleeve length, neckline depth, a body measurement or fabric. The flag is shown to you with the request, and the status changes to Waiting on details. This replaces the long call that a tailor would otherwise make to collect the same information. If you see a flag, look at the row it points to on the order card and fill it in.

## Chatting with a maker

Each request has a chat between you and the maker. Use it to answer a flagged question, share a clarification, or talk through a detail such as a fabric choice. You can write in English or Hinglish, just like in the main conversation. The chat belongs to that request, so everything about one garment stays together in one place. Keep the chat polite and about the garment, and keep sensitive things like banking details out of it.

## If a request is declined

A declined request means the maker will not take it on. Their reasons are the maker's own and may be about time, skills or the kind of garment. Declined does not mean the design is wrong. Your design and tailor sheet are still yours, and you can send the same design to a different maker or print the sheet and take it to a tailor of your choice.

## Maker accounts

Makers have a separate account from customers. A maker signs in on their own and sees an inbox of requests sent to them, with the tailor sheet and measurements for each. From the inbox, the maker can accept or decline a request, flag missing details and chat with the customer. A customer account does not give access to the maker inbox. If you are a tailor or designer and want to receive requests, use the maker sign-in instead of the customer one.

## The maker inbox

The maker inbox lists the requests that customers have sent to that maker, each with its status: New, Waiting on details, Accepted, Being made, Ready or Declined. Opening a request shows the tailor sheet part by part, next to the conversation with the customer. From there the maker can accept or decline the request, start making, mark the garment ready, or ask the customer for a detail that is missing.

## Moving a request forward

The maker moves a request through its statuses as they work: they accept it, update it to Being made when work begins, and mark it Ready when the garment is finished. The customer sees the current status of the request. If something changes, for example a missing detail turns up, the maker can flag it, and the customer is told through the request and the chat.
