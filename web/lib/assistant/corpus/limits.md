## What Bespoke does not do

Bespoke does not sell clothes. It does not stock garments, take orders for ready-made products or run a shop. It helps you describe a garment clearly so that a tailor or maker can create it. If you want to compare with ready-made options, the app offers plain search links to shopping sites, but buying happens on those sites and not in Bespoke.

## Prices and delivery

Bespoke does not give prices, quotes or delivery times. The cost of a garment and the time it takes depend on the maker, the fabric and the work involved, so ask the maker in the request chat. This assistant also does not know prices, so it cannot estimate them. If you ask about cost, the right step is to send the design to a maker and discuss it with them.

## Pictures are suggestions

A picture on the moodboard is a suggestion, not an instruction. Bespoke reads only garment type, colour and print from a whole picture, and sleeve and neckline from a circled part. A photograph cannot show your fit, your measurements or the exact fabric, so those still come from you. If a picture suggests something you do not want, say so in words or edit the order card and your choice replaces it.

## Where Bespoke can be wrong

Bespoke is built to be careful, but it can misread a message, especially an unusual phrase or heavy slang. It shows the words it understood and the value it chose for each part, so you can check and correct anything with a tap. The ease values on the tailor sheet are default starting values, not a promise about how the garment will fit, and your tailor may adjust them. Always confirm measurements and details with your tailor at the trial.

## What this assistant can help with

This assistant helps with Bespoke only: describing a garment, using the moodboard, taking measurements, reading the tailor sheet, understanding ease and neckline depth, and working with a maker. It does not give medical, legal or financial advice, and it does not handle programming, general knowledge or news. If a question is outside Bespoke, it says so politely. If the help notes do not contain the answer, it tells you it does not know.

## Where to get more help

If the assistant cannot answer, the most reliable next step for design questions is the main conversation, where Bespoke asks about each missing detail. For questions about a particular garment, such as a fabric or a stitching choice, ask the maker in the request chat or ask your tailor directly, because they know the practical limits of the work. Measurements are another good example: the guide in this assistant follows standard tailoring practice, but your tailor's own method comes first. Bespoke itself only produces the specification and the tailor sheet.
