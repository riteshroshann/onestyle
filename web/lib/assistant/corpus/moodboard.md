## What the moodboard is

The moodboard is a canvas where you pin pictures that show what you have in mind: a dress you saw, a sleeve you like, a colour. Bespoke reads the board together with your words and adds what it finds to the design. Pictures are suggestions. If a picture and your words disagree, your words win, and anything you edit or answer directly wins over both.

## Pinning pictures

Add a picture to the board and Bespoke numbers the pictures in reading order, left to right and top to bottom, so you can refer to them as picture 1, #2 and so on. Pin as many as you need. A whole picture gives Bespoke the garment type, the colour and the print, and only when one reading is clearly better than the others. If a picture is unclear or shows several things, Bespoke leaves those rows alone rather than guess.

## Circling a detail

To point at one part of a picture, draw a circle around it. A circled part tells Bespoke about the sleeve or the neckline in that picture. Bespoke looks at the circled area only, which is why a circle is more reliable than the whole picture for these two parts. A good habit is to circle the neckline you like on one picture and the sleeve you like on another, even if the garments differ.

## Notes with arrows

Write a short note on the board and connect it to a picture with an arrow, or place it right next to the picture. Bespoke attaches the note to the picture it points at. For example, a note saying "this neckline" with an arrow to a circled neckline tells Bespoke which part you want to copy. Notes can be in English or Hinglish, just like the main conversation.

## Saying "not this colour"

A negative note works as well as a positive one. If you like a dress but not its colour, write "not this colour" and attach it to the picture with an arrow. Bespoke then uses the garment and shape from the picture but will not take its colour, and it asks you for the colour you want instead. You can do the same with a print, a sleeve or a neckline by saying "not this sleeve" or "not this print".

## Comparing pictures: "deeper than #1"

You can compare pictures instead of giving numbers. A note such as "neckline deeper than #1" or "deeper than #1 but less than #2" is turned into a range in inches using the neckline depths of those pictures. Bespoke proposes a value toward the shallow end of the range, because a neckline can be deepened at the trial but never raised. If the pictures contradict each other, for example if #2 is shallower than #1 but you want the neckline between them, Bespoke asks you instead of guessing.

## What a whole picture gives and what a circle gives

A whole picture gives garment type, colour and print. A circled part gives sleeve and neckline. Other details, such as fit, length and fabric, are not read from pictures, because a photograph cannot show them reliably; Bespoke asks you for those or uses your words. This keeps the specification honest: only what a picture can really show is taken from it.

## Pictures suggest, words win

Everything read from a picture is marked on the order card as a suggestion from the board. If you later say something different in words, such as "make it green", the green replaces what the picture suggested. If you tap a row on the order card and pick a value, that choice replaces both. The order in which Bespoke trusts things is: your edit on the order card, your answer to a question, your words, a note on the board, a picture, and finally any stored profile.
