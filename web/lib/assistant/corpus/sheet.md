## The tailor sheet

The tailor sheet is a printable page that lists every instruction for the garment, part by part: garment, neckline shape and depth, sleeves, fit, length, fabric, colour, details and any notes. Each line shows where it came from, so a tailor can tell your own words from a default. It prints to A4 or saves as a PDF. Drawings of the chosen neckline, sleeves, shape and length are shown beside the instructions where drawings are available. The sheet appears in the conversation once the design is complete.

## English and Hindi sheets

You can switch the tailor sheet between English and Hindi. The switch is complete: part names, values, labels and the finished measurements all change language, for example neckline becomes गले का आकार and sleeve length becomes आस्तीन की लंबाई. This helps when your tailor reads Hindi more comfortably than English. You can print either version, and you can print both to keep one for yourself.

## Finished measurement equals body plus ease

The sheet shows finished measurements, which are the size of the garment once it is sewn, not the size of your body. A finished measurement is your body measurement plus ease, the extra room a fit needs. A fitted bodice has less ease and a loose one has more. Bespoke adds the ease for the fit you chose to the body measurements you entered, so the tailor can cut directly from the sheet without doing the sum.

## Ease: what it means

Ease is the extra room between your body and the garment. Without ease, a garment would be exactly as big as your body and you could not breathe, sit or lift your arms comfortably. Ease depends on the fit you ask for. The more relaxed the fit, the more ease the tailor adds to your measurement. Ease is added to the bust, waist and hip according to the fit chosen for the upper and lower parts of the garment.

## Ease numbers Bespoke uses

Bespoke uses these default ease allowances, in inches: fitted 1.5, semi-fitted 2.5, relaxed 3.5 and loose 5. For example, a fitted kurti with a 34 inch bust comes out at 35.5 inches finished, and a loose one at 39 inches. These are starting values shown as defaults on the sheet, to be replaced by partner tailors' own values over time. If your tailor works differently, tell them; the sheet marks the ease as a default so that they know it is adjustable.

## Fit words and what they mean

Bespoke uses four fits. Fitted is close to the body, with the least ease, similar to words like snug, tight or body hugging. Semi-fitted is slightly eased, like a regular fit. Relaxed is comfortable and easy, and not tight. Loose is roomy, including words like dheela, baggy, flowy, oversized or boxy. You can give a fit for the upper body and a different one for the lower part, such as fitted at the waist and loose below, and you can soften it with "thoda".

## Neckline depth: what it means

Neckline depth is how far down the front neckline drops. Bespoke uses four depths in inches: shallow 4.5, moderate 6, deep 7.5 and plunging 9. A word like "thoda deep" falls between moderate and deep, which is about 6.75 inches. Neckline depth is one of the most important details to get right, because a neckline that is cut too deep cannot be raised again, while one that is too shallow can be deepened at the trial. This is why Bespoke asks about it early and proposes the shallower value when it is unsure.

## Neckline shapes

Besides depth, a neckline has a shape. Bespoke knows round (gol gala), V, deep V, boat, square (chaukor), sweetheart, halter, high neck, mandarin collar, keyhole, off-shoulder, U, collar, cowl, potli, crew, hooded and turtle necks. Pick the shape first and the depth second. A round neck and a deep V can both be described as deep, but they look very different. If you are not sure, circle a neckline on a picture in the moodboard and Bespoke will suggest the nearest one.

## Sleeve length and style

Sleeve length is separate from sleeve style. Bespoke has six lengths: sleeveless, cap, short, elbow, three-quarter and full. Styles include puff, bell, bishop (also called balloon), flutter, cold shoulder, kimono (also batwing or dolman), raglan, straight and sheer. Sleeve length is asked about because a sleeve cut too short cannot be lengthened. You can say, for example, "three-quarter sleeves, bell style" or "baazu chhote".

## Garment length

Garment length is how far down the garment reaches. Bespoke uses seven steps from shortest to longest: crop, hip, short, knee, midi, ankle and floor. In Hinglish, "ghutne tak" means to the knee, "lamba" or "lambi" means long and "chhota" means short. Length is asked about because fabric that is cut short cannot be made longer. If you are unsure, tell the tailor the point on your body where you want the hem to end, and use a picture as a guide.

## What the sheet does not contain

The tailor sheet is a specification, not a price quote or an order confirmation. It does not include a price, a delivery date or a guarantee about the result. Finished measurements depend on the body measurements you give. Bring the sheet to your tailor and let them confirm the details at the trial. If something on the sheet looks wrong, go back to the order card, tap the row, pick the right value and open the sheet again; the sheet always reflects the current design, so there is nothing to fix on the printed page itself.
