What it does

A customer says kurti chahiye, waist pe fitted, neeche se thoda loose, V neck thoda deep, no embroidery. Bespoke turns that into a part-by-part specification: a fitted bodice with 1.5 inches of ease, a slightly loose hem, a V neckline about 6.75 inches deep, and no embroidery. It then asks for the sleeve length, fabric and colour, ranked by what a wrong guess would cost.

The design ends as a tailor sheet in English or Hindi, ideas and shopping searches, or a request sent to a designer or tailor on Bespoke, who can flag what is missing and talk it through in one thread.

Understanding that does not break

Two readers work on every message. On the device, always: a gazetteer of 1,212 forms in English, Hinglish and Devanagari and a fine-tuned 33M-parameter tagger running in the browser as int8 ONNX. Alongside it, when configured: a language model whose every value is checked against the ontology. If the model is slow or down, the device answer stands and nobody sees an error.

The deployed tagger, gazetteer first with the transformer filling the gaps, scores 0.953 span F1 on held-out requests, with recall of 0.967. Every number is reproducible from the repository.

Scores are strict seqeval span F1 on 52 hand-written requests with 95% bootstrap intervals. The set is small, and the developer guide says exactly how small.
