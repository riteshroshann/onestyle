Using
Bespoke.

Four rooms in the studio, one thread with a maker, and an adviser in the corner. Here is what each is for and how to get the most from it.

Talk

Open /studio/talk/ and say what you want the way you would tell a friend: the garment, who it is for, the occasion, colours, anything you already know.

  1. Describe it, typed or spoken. A maroon silk kurta for my brother's wedding, mandarin collar, no heavy work. Press the microphone to speak in English, Hindi or Hinglish: the clip is transcribed in Latin script and lands in the box for you to check before it is sent. A clip with no voice in it is never turned into words.
  2. Answer one question at a time. The studio asks the single question that matters most next, with options to tap, a Skip and room to type your own answer. It never asks the same thing twice. Who it is for and the age range come first, because they change the cut and the shopping.
  3. Change anything. Every detail sits in the panel on the right with where it came from, including who it is for, age, budget and style. Select one to change it. Back undoes a step.

Messages that are not about clothes, or that try to change the studio's rules, get a kind reply with example briefs instead of a guessed garment. Logged in, your design is saved to your account and opens again on any device. Logged out, it lasts for the browser tab, and it is kept on the device for two hours so it survives creating an account.

Board

Some things are easier to show than to say. /studio/board/ opens blank every time; earlier boards are kept in its history, top left.

  1. Drop anything you love. Garments, but also anime, illustration, architecture, abstract art or a landscape.
  2. Mark what matters. Circle a sleeve, or write a note such as this neckline, not this colour or deeper than #1.
  3. Read the board. Each picture is read for what is worth borrowing: palette, motifs, mood, prints and silhouettes. The ideas are written onto the board as notes next to each picture, with a direction of two to four garment ideas.
  4. Choose what happens next. Use a direction to take it into Talk, circle a detail for a closer look, or keep the notes on the board. Your words always win over pictures.

Sheet

The tailor sheet lists every part in plain instructions, in English or Hindi, with drawings and where each choice came from. Add bust, waist, hip, shoulder, length and sleeve, and the finished garment measurements are worked out with the right ease for the fit you chose. Print it, save it as a PDF, or send it to a maker.

Shop

Searches written from your whole design: something close ready-made, ideas on Pinterest, the fabric by the metre, and tailors near you. If the design is for a man, every shop opens its men's section; for a boy, its boys' section; and so on, so you never wade through the wrong results. Every link opens on the shop's own site, and Bespoke never stores what you find there.

Makers and the inbox

Designers, tailors and boutiques join through For makers. A customer sends a design from the sheet, logging in first if needed, and both sides then share one inbox.

  1. One thread per design. The design sits pinned at the top of the thread, with the full sheet a tap away.
  2. Makers run the work. They move a request through new, needs info, accepted, in progress, ready or declined, flag a missing detail, and reply with one tap from quick replies.
  3. Nobody misses a message. Unread counts sit on the Inbox link and in the tab title, a note pops up wherever you are on the site, and the other side sees when you are typing.

Ask

The button in the corner answers two kinds of question. How Bespoke works, from its own guides, with the guide named under the answer and a button to the right page. And what to choose: palettes, fabrics, necklines, what suits an occasion, using the design you have open.

It refuses everything else: other topics, attempts to change its rules, medical, legal or money advice. Emails and phone numbers are masked before anything reaches the model, and no answer may contain a link that is not in Bespoke's own guides.
