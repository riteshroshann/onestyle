Using
Bespoke.

Four rooms in the studio, one thread with a maker, and an adviser in the corner. Here is what each is for and how to get the most from it.

Talk

Say what you want the way you would tell a friend: the garment, who it is for, the occasion, colours, anything you already know. Type it, or press the microphone and speak in English or Hindi.

  1. Describe it. A maroon silk kurta for my brother's wedding, mandarin collar, no heavy work. Every word the studio understood is underlined; hover to see how it was read.
  2. Answer a few questions. It asks only for what is missing, most costly first. A neckline is asked early because it can be deepened at the trial but never raised.
  3. Change anything. Every detail sits in the panel on the right with where it came from. Select one to change it. Back undoes a step.

Signed out, your design lasts for the browser tab. Logged in, it is saved to your account and opens again on any device.

Board

Some things are easier to show than to say. The board opens blank every time; earlier boards are kept in its history, top left.

  1. Drop pictures. One look per picture works best.
  2. Mark what matters. Circle a sleeve, or write a note such as this neckline, not this colour or deeper than #1.
  3. Read the board. Colour, print and garment come from whole pictures; sleeve and neckline only from what you circle. Your words always win over pictures.

Sheet

The tailor sheet lists every part in plain instructions, in English or Hindi, with drawings and where each choice came from. Add bust, waist, hip, shoulder, length and sleeve, and the finished garment measurements are worked out with the right ease for the fit you chose. Print it, save it as a PDF, or send it to a maker.

Shop

Searches written from your design: something close ready-made, ideas on Pinterest, the fabric by the metre, and tailors near you. Every link opens on the shop's own site; Bespoke never stores what you find there.

Makers

Designers, tailors and boutiques join through For makers. A customer sends a design from the sheet; the maker sees the sheet and measurements, flags any missing detail, moves the request through new, accepted, in progress and ready, and talks it through in one thread.

Ask

The button in the corner answers two kinds of question. How Bespoke works, from its own guides, with the guide named under the answer and a button to the right page. And what to choose: palettes, fabrics, necklines, what suits an occasion, using the design you have open.

It refuses everything else: other topics, attempts to change its rules, medical, legal or money advice. Emails and phone numbers are masked before anything reaches the model, and no answer may contain a link that is not in Bespoke's own guides.
